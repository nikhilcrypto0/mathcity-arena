## Q2. How the agency arrangement works: contract length, conversion, benefits, weekly pay

### Takeaway
Data center technician contracts are usually posted as "contract" or "contract-to-hire" with "extensions and potential conversion." I found no source giving a standard length for these exact roles. Published conversion statistics vary widely and many come from low-quality aggregators, so treat any conversion number as soft.

### Cited Findings
- Insight Global DC tech contracts are described as running with extensions and potential conversion to permanent — [ZipRecruiter Insight Global DC Tech](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/c/Insight-Global/Job/Datacenter-Technician/-in-Des-Moines,IA?jid=28ed82463f49e5e2)
- Lorien entry-level DC tech contracts are described as having "strong potential for conversion" to permanent full-time — [Lorien job 636987](https://www.lorienglobal.com/us/jobs/636987-Entry-Level-Data-Center-Technician)
- Conversion statistics conflict:
  - Several staffing-firm blogs attribute to the American Staffing Association (ASA) a claim that "nearly 60%" of temp and contract workers move into permanent jobs. This figure counts permanent jobs anywhere, not only at the client — [KORE1](https://www.kore1.com/contract-to-hire-conversion-rate/); [Midwest Staffing](https://midweststaffing.com/temp-to-perm-rates-2026/)
  - Staffing Industry Analysts (SIA) research on large employers (1,000+ employees) puts the median share of the temp workforce converted per year in North America at about 10% — same sources as above.
  - Further claims that temp-to-hire placements were "up 23% in 2025" and that the "conversion rate reached 34%" also trace only to secondary staffing-firm blogs — [onsitepersonnel](https://onsitepersonnel.com/temp-to-perm-a-growing-trend-in-2026/); [activatedscale](https://www.activatedscale.com/blog/temp-to-perm-conversion-benefits-success-rate). These are unverified.
- Aerotek runs contractor self-service pages for online pay and W-2s, which confirms that Aerotek is the W-2 employer of record for its contractors — [Aerotek Online Pay & W-2](https://www.aerotek.com/en/career-opportunities/contractors/contractor-resources/online-pay-w2); [Aerotek employment verification](https://www.aerotek.com/en/career-opportunities/contractors/contractor-resources/employment-verification)

### Inferences
- For planning, a realistic expectation is "contract of unspecified length with possible conversion." Conversion at a specific hyperscaler or OEM is far less likely than the 60% headline implies. The SIA median of about 10% is the more conservative benchmark.
- For an OPT holder, conversion to the client changes the employer. That needs a new SEVP Portal report on OPT, and on STEM OPT a new I-983 plus an E-Verify check of the new employer (see Q4).

## Q3. Regular (post-completion) OPT rules when working through an agency

### Takeaway
On regular 12-month OPT, a W-2 staffing agency is an acceptable employer. The job must be directly related to the major and at least 20 hours per week. Unemployment is capped at 90 days, and the student must report employer details (including the actual worksite) in the SEVP Portal within 10 days of starting or of any change.

### Cited Findings
- Unemployment cap: no more than 90 days of unemployment during the initial 12-month OPT. Reaching 90 days, or 90 days without reported employment, can lead SEVP to terminate the SEVIS record — [Temple ISSS](https://global.temple.edu/isss/students/current-students/f-1-student/f-1-employment-options/campus-employment/optional-practical-training-opt/post-completion-opt/opt-and-unemployment); [EduConnect 2026](https://educonnectusa.com/2026/08/opt-unemployment-days/)
- Directly related: the student must make sure the job is directly related to the major and must describe how in the SEVP Portal — [U Rochester ISO](https://www.rochester.edu/iso/current-students/working/f-1-opt/reporting/); [UB ISS](https://www.buffalo.edu/international-student-services/immigration-visa/f-1-student/optional-practical-training--opt-/maintaining-status-during-post-completion-opt.html)
- Hours: post-completion OPT employment must be at least 20 hours per week to count as employment — [UW Bothell](https://uwbdr.uwb.edu/international-student-services/f-1-benefits/practical-training/post-completion-opt/post-completion-opt-requirements-and-faq)
- Agency employment: work through an agency or consulting firm is a valid form of employment on OPT. The student should be able to show an average of 20+ hours per week while employed by the agency — [UTA OPT reporting](https://uta.edu/student-affairs/oie/isss/employment/opt-categories-simplified/reporting); [WashU OISS](https://oiss.washu.edu/while-on-opt/)
- Post-completion OPT allows self-employment, temp-agency employment and contract employment. STEM OPT requires a bona fide employer-employee relationship — [Harvard HIO STEM OPT Employment Types](https://www.hio.harvard.edu/node/50457); [BU ISSO](https://www.bu.edu/isso/employment-internships/student-off-campus-work-and-training/24-month-stem-opt/employment-types/)
- Reporting: within 10 days, report the employer name, the employer address (the actual work location), full-time vs part-time status, start and end dates, and how the job relates to the field of study — [UTA](https://uta.edu/student-affairs/oie/isss/employment/opt-categories-simplified/reporting); [Study in the States – SEVIS Help Hub OPT employer](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-help-hub/student-records/fm-student-employment/f-1-add-edit-delete-optional-practical)

### Inferences
- On regular OPT, the agency (for example Aerotek) is the employer to report. The worksite address should be the client data center or factory where the student actually works, not the agency's branch office. Confirm this convention with the DSO.
- Unemployment days keep counting during any gap between contracts, so back-to-back agency assignments matter.

## Q4. STEM OPT rules for agencies (E-Verify, I-983, training, bona fide relationship, 150 days, site visits, commensurate pay)

### Takeaway
A staffing agency can be a STEM OPT employer only if it (1) is enrolled in E-Verify, (2) is the bona fide employer, (3) itself provides and supervises the training described on Form I-983, and (4) pays what similarly situated US workers earn. It cannot hand training off to the client site's staff. 2026 brought heavy enforcement aimed specifically at IT consulting and staffing firms. Separately, the 150-day aggregate unemployment limit applies across the full OPT plus STEM OPT period (general knowledge; it did not surface in a snippet this session, so verify).

### Cited Findings
- **Agencies allowed, with conditions:** staffing and temp agencies may employ STEM OPT students only if the agency provides the practical training experience and keeps a bona fide employer-employee relationship — [SJSU ISSS Allowable Employment](https://www.sjsu.edu/isss/current-students/employment/stem/employment.php); [Study in the States STEM OPT Overview](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/stem-opt-hub/additional-resources/stem-opt-extension-overview)
- **Who signs the I-983:** if a temp or staffing agency places the student, the agency cannot sign the I-983 unless it is the E-Verified employer providing the actual training. Only the E-Verified employer providing that training may complete and sign it. Multiple short placements with different employers need a new I-983 for each — [Study in the States DSO Q&A: Can STEM OPT students use staffing or temporary agencies?](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/2016/07/questions-designated-school-officials-can-stem-opt-students-use-staffing-or-temporary-0); [CU Boulder ISSS](https://www.colorado.edu/isss/2018/06/11/24-month-stem-extensions-and-staffing-or-temporary-agency-employers)
- **No delegation of training:** the people who provide and supervise training may be the employer's own employees or contractors the employer has directly retained. They may not be employees or contractors of the employer's clients or customers. A STEM OPT employer may not assign or delegate its training responsibilities to a non-employer third party such as a client — [USCIS STEM OPT page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-extension-for-stem-students-stem-opt); [Reddy Neumann Brown](https://www.rnlawgroup.com/stem-opt-compliance-what-employers-need-to-know/)
- **Case-by-case review:** USCIS reviews case by case whether the student is a bona fide employee of the I-983 signer, and whether the signer is the same entity that employs the student and provides the training — [NYU STEM OPT FAQs](https://www.nyu.edu/students/student-information-and-resources/student-visa-and-immigration/alumni/extend-your-opt/stem-opt/FAQs-on-the-new-regulations-for-STEM-OPT.html); [Dartmouth OVIS](https://ovis-intl.dartmouth.edu/immigration/f-1-students/employment/stem-optional-practical-training-opt/types-employment)
- **Arrangements that may not qualify:** multiple-employer arrangements, sole proprietorships, temp-agency employment, labor-for-hire consulting and similar setups may fail to show a bona fide relationship — [New Paltz ISS](https://www.newpaltz.edu/iss/students-currently-on-opt--stem-opt/employment-possibilities-for-opt-stem-extension/); [Dartmouth OVIS](https://ovis-intl.dartmouth.edu/immigration/f-1-students/employment/stem-optional-practical-training-opt/types-employment)
- **Third-party worksites:** in 2018 USCIS briefly stated a restriction on training at client sites, then clarified that STEM OPT training can take place at third-party locations as long as the employer keeps its training and supervision obligations — [Global Immigration Blog (2018)](https://www.globalimmigrationblog.com/2018/04/uscis-introduces-onsite-training-restriction-on-staffing-agencies-hiring-opt-stem-students/); [Gokare Law](https://gokarelaw.com/uscis-clarifies-that-stem-opt-students-may-engage-in-training-at-third-party-locations/)
- **Pay:** the student must be paid the same as similarly situated US workers — [New Paltz ISS](https://www.newpaltz.edu/iss/students-currently-on-opt--stem-opt/employment-possibilities-for-opt-stem-extension/); [NYU employer info](https://www.nyu.edu/students/student-information-and-resources/student-visa-and-immigration/alumni/extend-your-opt/stem-opt/information-for-stem-opt-employers.html)
- **E-Verify:** E-Verify enrollment is required of the employer and cannot be met by the student. The employer's E-Verify account must be active, and its EIN must match the I-983 — [Reddy Neumann Brown – E-Verify](https://www.rnlawgroup.com/e-verify-requirements-for-stem-opt-extensions-and-i-9-compliance/)
- **Site visits:** ICE generally gives 48 hours' notice of a STEM OPT site visit, but no notice is needed when there is a complaint or evidence of noncompliance. Visits involve inspecting the worksite, interviewing the student and supervisors, and comparing documents with SEVIS — [Fragomen – STEM OPT Worksite Inspections](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/stem-opt-worksite-inspections-what-employers-need-to-know.html); [Reddy Neumann Brown](https://www.rnlawgroup.com/ices-opt-fraud-crackdown-a-compliance-guide-for-employers-of-f-1-students/)
- **2026 enforcement:**
  - On Mar 23, 2026, SEVP Broadcast Message 2603-02 ("STEM OPT Employer Fraud") said SEVP and DHS partners were investigating fraud among "IT recruitment, consulting firms, and staffing agencies." Indicators included unoccupied offices, residential addresses used as worksites, offshore HR and payroll, and students who never showed up for work — [ICE BCM 2603-02](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/bcm260302.pdf); [MIT ISO summary](https://iso.mit.edu/news/sevp-issues-broadcast-message-regarding-prevention-of-stem-opt-employer-fraud/)
  - On May 12, 2026, ICE said it had identified more than 10,000 students claiming employment with "highly suspect employers," based on a review of the top 25 OPT employers. Site visits took place in TX, VA, GA, IL, NY, NJ, NC and FL — [Adhikari Law](https://www.adhikarilaw.com/news-and-alerts/news-insights/trump-administration-news-insights/immigration-and-customs-enforcement-ice-releases-information-on-alleged-fraud-related-to-the-24-month-opt-extension-for-stem-f-1-students/); [Ellis – OPT Crackdown 2026](https://www.ellis.com/resources/opt-crackdown-2026)

### Inferences
- Large, legitimate W-2 agencies with real US offices and payroll (the Allegis firms, Insight Global, Kforce, Apex) are structurally much safer than the small "OPT consulting" or bench-marketing firms that the 2026 enforcement targeted.
- Even a large agency must show that its own personnel supervise and evaluate the student on the I-983. A client's data center lead acting as the de facto trainer is the classic compliance failure. Ask the recruiter who at the agency will sign the I-983 evaluations.

### Gaps
- No snippet this session quoted the 150-day STEM OPT unemployment aggregate or the 12-month/final self-evaluation and 6-month validation reporting from a primary source. These are well established in 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(E) and the STEM OPT Hub, but the writer should cite studyinthestates.dhs.gov directly.

## Q6. "US citizens only," "no C2C," and "authorized without sponsorship" postings — impact and disclosure tips

### Takeaway
"No C2C" isn't a barrier for OPT holders, who must be W-2 employees anyway. "Must be authorized without sponsorship now or in the future" usually screens out anyone who will later need H-1B, and "US citizens only" (often tied to clearance or export control) excludes OPT holders entirely. Answer honestly: state current OPT authorization with its end date and the possibility of STEM OPT. Misrepresentation is far riskier than disclosure.

### Cited Findings
- If the EAD is valid for the start date, "Are you currently authorized to work?" can be answered yes. Merely being eligible to apply for OPT is not the same thing — [TrackMyOPT – sponsorship question](https://www.trackmyopt.com/blog/how-to-answer-sponsorship-question); [CMU OIE – Work authorization questions](https://www.cmu.edu/oie/employment/resources/work-authorization.html)
- On a "now or in the future" sponsorship question, answer Yes if you expect to need sponsorship after OPT. Do not check "no" when the true answer is yes, because misrepresentation is worse than needing sponsorship — [TrackMyOPT](https://www.trackmyopt.com/blog/how-to-answer-sponsorship-question); [f1jobs.io](https://www.f1jobs.io/resources/blog/answer-do-you-need-sponsorship-interview)
- Suggested phrasing: "I am currently authorized to work through [date] under F-1 OPT. My current OPT does not require the company to file a work-visa petition, but I may need sponsorship for a longer-term status in the future." — [TrackMyOPT](https://www.trackmyopt.com/blog/how-to-answer-sponsorship-question)
- Context: DHS has proposed a permanent $103,265 H-1B fee, which raises the cost of OPT-to-H-1B sponsorship for employers — [Fakhoury Law](https://fakhouryglobal.com/immigration-alerts/fgi-alert-dhs-proposes-permanent-103265-h-1b-fee-potentially-expanding-costs-for-opt-to-h-1b-sponsorship/); [TrackMyOPT](https://www.trackmyopt.com/blog/dhs-proposes-103265-h1b-fee-2026)

### Inferences
- Practical tips (my inference, not sourced):
  - Tell the agency recruiter early that you are on OPT (or STEM OPT eligible) and give the EAD end date.
  - Ask whether the agency is in E-Verify and will sign an I-983 if your STEM OPT is coming up.
  - Ask whether the client allows OPT/EAD workers. Some clients bar non-citizens on export-control or clearance grounds.
- Hourly technician roles paying $22–$30/hr are unlikely candidates for H-1B sponsorship, especially given the proposed H-1B fee. The OPT/STEM OPT window may be the full horizon for this route unless the role leads into engineering positions.

### Gaps
- No data on how many data center technician postings carry "US citizen only" or "no sponsorship" language. Hyperscaler sites with government or security work may require citizenship, but this is unverified.

## Q7. Is a technician role "directly related" to MS CS / MS IS / MS EE / BS Electronics?

### Takeaway
Whether a job is directly related is decided case by case, by the student's own explanation and the DSO's review. Hardware debug, test and rack-integration roles map most naturally onto EE and electronics degrees. CS and IS degrees can qualify if the duties stress systems, networking, firmware or diagnostics. Purely physical "rack and stack" or logistics duties are the weakest case, especially at master's level, where the job should fit both the field and the degree level.

### Cited Findings
- OPT jobs should be "in your field and at your level": directly related to the major and requiring the student's level of education — [migratemate – DC technician OPT jobs](https://migratemate.co/opt-jobs/data-center-technician) (commercial job site, secondary)
- Data center technician work (hardware installation, server maintenance, network infrastructure) is described as aligning with CS and IT programs. Confirm with the DSO before accepting — [migratemate – Data Center OPT jobs](https://migratemate.co/opt-jobs/data-center) (commercial, secondary)
- JHU OIS publishes sample "how this job relates to my degree" explanations for post-completion OPT reporting — [JHU sample OPT explanations (PDF)](https://ois.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/sample_OPT_explanations.pdf)
- The SEVP Portal requires a description of how the job relates to the field of study — [U Rochester ISO](https://www.rochester.edu/iso/current-students/working/f-1-opt/reporting/)

### Inferences
- Degree-by-degree (inference):
  - **MS EE and BS Electronics:** strongest fit for server debug, test, failure analysis and power/thermal validation.
  - **MS CS:** good fit if the role involves OS, BIOS or firmware diagnostics, scripting test automation, or Linux and networking troubleshooting.
  - **MS IS:** moderate fit for DC operations, asset and ticketing, or network roles.
  - **All degrees:** weakest fit for entry-level "no experience required" roles, such as Lorien's entry-level postings, when the student holds a master's degree.
- For STEM OPT, the I-983 has to spell out learning objectives tied to the STEM degree. A thin technician role makes that plan harder to write convincingly.

### Gaps
- Found no DSO-authored guidance specifically addressing data center or hardware technician roles. Sources here are commercial job boards plus generic university reporting guidance. Ask your DSO.

## 2025–2026 policy changes to watch (cross-cutting)

### Cited Findings
- On Jan 9, 2026, DHS confirmed it is re-evaluating the scope and duration of OPT and STEM OPT. The Unified Regulatory Agenda lists "Practical Training" (RIN 1653-AA97) to address "fraud and national security concerns, protect U.S. workers." No final rule exists. Rulemaking usually takes 12–24+ months — [Erickson Immigration Group](https://eiglaw.com/dhs-confirms-review-of-opt-and-stem-opt/); [Constangy](https://www.constangy.com/newsroom/newsletters/dhs-to-propose-overhaul-of-opt-program-for-international-students); [TrackMyOPT](https://www.trackmyopt.com/blog/is-opt-ending-dhs-rule-2026)
- On Aug 20, 2026, ICE sent an "Optional Practical Training Fees" proposed rule to OMB. OIRA finished review on Sep 11, 2026 ("Consistent with Change"), but the rule had not been published as of the sources. Media reports of a possible $100,000 fee are unconfirmed. The current I-765 fee is $470 online or $520 by mail — [Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-dhs-optional-practical-training-fee-proposal-clears-federal-review.html); [UIC OIS](https://ois.uic.edu/news-stories/dhs-advances-proposed-rulemaking-on-opt-fees/); [Maggio Kattar](https://maggio-kattar.com/latest-changes-us-immigration-law/opt-fee-proposal-clears-white-house-review-publication-expected-next/)
- On Jul 17, 2026, DHS published a final rule replacing duration of status with fixed admission periods, effective Sep 15, 2026. F-1 students are admitted until the I-20 program end date (maximum 4 years) or the OPT/STEM OPT EAD end date, whichever is earlier, plus a 30-day grace period. Students who timely file an I-765 for OPT or STEM OPT by Mar 18, 2027 don't need a separate extension-of-stay filing — [Federal Register 2026-14439](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/17/2026-14439/establishing-a-fixed-time-period-of-admission-and-an-extension-of-stay-procedure-for-nonimmigrant); [Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/07/17/dhs-finalizes-rule-replacing-duration-of-status-for-f-1-j-1-and-i-visa-holders); [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2806/2026-07-23-dhs-issues-final-rule-creating-fixed-period-admission-f)
- H.R. 2315 is a bill that would end OPT. It is not law — [VisaVerge](https://www.visaverge.com/news/opt-restrictions-move-forward-h-r-2315-aims-to-end-opt/)
- The 2026 crackdown on staffing and consulting OPT employers is covered under Q4: [ICE BCM 2603-02](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/bcm260302.pdf)

### Inferences
- Three things together make agency-based OPT employment riskier in late 2026 than in prior years: enforcement focused on staffing and consulting firms, a pending OPT fee rule, and a pending "Practical Training" rule. Choosing a large, established W-2 agency with real supervision, and keeping meticulous SEVP Portal reporting, is the main way to reduce that risk.

### Gaps
- The text and fee amounts of the OPT Fees NPRM and the Practical Training NPRM are not public as of the sources found. Re-check the Federal Register before the final report.
